# HW7 tuning round: findings (AGE-38)

Run `tuning-r1` (jobs j01–j08, 2 trial repeats each). The numbers come from `report.md` and the
CSVs in this folder, all generated by
`uv run python -m experiments.hw7.analyze --runs tuning-r1`. This page is the interpretation.
Definitions are in `experiments/hw7/PREREG.md`.

## Headline

| Method | Mean SQ [95% CI over jobs] | Hit rate | Bad picks | Cost per correct invoice | Time to hire |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| **Trial-then-Hire (6)** | **0.925** [0.864, 0.977] | 56% | 1/8 | $0.0143 | ≤ 71 s |
| Profile-reading | 0.941 [0.894, 0.981] | 78% | 0/8 | $0.0096 | 2.9 s |
| Cheapest | 0.710 [0.397, 0.952] | 62% | 3/8 | $0.0011 | 0 |
| Random (expected) | 0.848 [0.763, 0.927] | 55% | 3/8 | $0.0046 | 0 |

- Trial-then-Hire minus profile-reading is **−0.017**, with a paired-bootstrap 95% CI of
  [−0.087, +0.063].
- Against PREREG §7, this is **provisionally not supported** (8 of the 12 jobs):
  - Criterion 1 (SQ ≥ 0.90) is **met**: 0.925.
  - Criterion 2 (beat profile-reading by ≥ 0.05, CI above 0) is **not met**.
  - The binding verdict needs the 4 fresh jobs' before-change results (AGE-40).
- Trial-then-Hire clearly beats cheapest and random. It does not beat profile-reading, which is
  already strong on these jobs (0.941).

## Trial size (3 / 6 / 12)

| Size | Mean SQ | Hit rate | Tie rate | Trial cost per job |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 3 | 0.899 | 44% | 100% | $0.06 |
| 6 | 0.925 | 56% | 100% | $0.13 |
| 12 | 0.936 | 62% | 100% | $0.27 |

- Each doubling of the trial adds less SQ than the one before (+0.026, then +0.012) and doubles
  the cost. None of the three sizes reaches profile-reading's 0.941.
- **Every verdict at every size is a tie** (both repeats, all 200 draws). A bigger trial only
  shrinks the tie group, from 6.4 of 7 providers at size 3 to 5.6 at size 6 and 5.1 at size 12.

## The failure: the trial measures accuracy well, but the Ranker lets cost decide

1. **The trial ranks providers well.** Across providers, trial accuracy correlates with held-out
   (real) accuracy, with Spearman ρ between 0.72 and 0.99 on all 8 jobs.
2. **The Ranker discards most of that ranking.**
   - A provider is tied with the leader when its 95% CI overlaps the leader's. Many providers score
     at or near 100% on the trial, so the CIs of all but the clearly worse providers reach the
     leader's. That makes every verdict a tie, with a tie group of about 5–6 out of 7 providers.
   - Inside the tie group, the pick is the lowest cost per document. At 6 invoices, **30% of
     verdicts pick a provider with lower trial accuracy than the leader** (25% at size 3, 38% at
     size 12).
   - This is where the SQ is lost:
     - j02 (real invoices): 0.758, the only bad pick
     - j03 (scanned): 0.833
     - j01 (near-tie job): 0.917
     - j04 (scanned): 0.898
3. **Narrowing the tie test doesn't fix it (what-if).** Suppose a tie meant "the paired bootstrap
   CI of the per-document difference from the leader includes 0", with the same cost tie-break.
   The tie rate stays at 100% and SQ at size 6 only moves from 0.925 to 0.930. The ties are real
   (several providers really do score 100% on the trial), so changing the statistical test doesn't
   help. Letting cost override accuracy inside the group is what costs SQ.
4. **Picking the trial leader instead (what-if).**
   - SQ at size 6 goes from 0.925 to **0.959**, hit rate from 56% to **78%**, and bad picks from
     1 to **0**. The gain holds at every size: 0.946 at 3, 0.962 at 12.
   - Picking the leader minus profile-reading is +0.018, with a 95% CI of [−0.034, +0.081] (from
     `analyze.analyse`, not in the generated tables). So this change alone would probably still
     miss criterion 2 on these 8 jobs.

Both what-ifs are exploratory: they are not pre-registered, and they were chosen after seeing the
tuning jobs. The fresh jobs (j09–j12) are the test of any change.

## A second limit: the tuning jobs leave little headroom

- On 7 of the 8 jobs, the best held-out accuracy is 1.000. On 6 of them, 3 to 5 providers are
  within 0.02 of the best (`report.md`, trial-vs-held-out table).
- So random already gets 0.848 and profile-reading 0.941. A margin of ≥ 0.05 over
  profile-reading needs Trial-then-Hire at ≥ 0.99. That is close to the ceiling and can only come
  from the few jobs where providers really differ (j02, j03, j04).
- This is a property of the job set, not something to fix. Report it as a limitation.

## Real vs synthetic (descriptive, only 2 jobs)

- On j02, the held-out invoices are real (DocILE). Its trial has 3 real samples and 9 synthetic
  invoices.
- j02 is Trial-then-Hire's worst job: SQ 0.758 at size 6, and 0.701 / 0.758 / 0.794 at sizes
  3 / 6 / 12. Profile-reading scores 0.933 there.
- On the synthetic clean job j01, Trial-then-Hire scores 0.917. On the other 6 synthetic jobs it
  averages 0.954.
- j02 is also the hardest job overall: the best provider's real accuracy is only 0.743, and two
  providers are within 0.02 of it.
- Picking the leader would raise j02 to 0.873.
- With j09 still to come, this is the clearest sign that a mostly synthetic trial transfers less
  well to real invoices.

## Run-to-run noise (r1 vs r2, 12 invoices)

- The two repeats picked the same provider on **4 of 8 jobs** (j03, j05, j06, j08).
- Where the picks differ, the SQ gap is 0.013–0.167 (largest on j01, the near-tie job).
- With the cost tie-break deciding among 5–6 tied providers, which provider wins the tie-break
  varies with the synthetic invoices drawn.

## Proposed changes for AGE-39 (pick one)

1. **Recommended: an accuracy-first tie-break in the Ranker.**
   - Inside the tie group, recommend the provider with the highest trial accuracy. Use cost per
     document, then latency, only to break ties in accuracy (exact ties, or within a small margin
     such as 0.01 that is fixed before the retest).
   - Keep the tie status and the tied list as they are, so the requester still sees that the
     providers are statistically close.
   - What-if on tuning: SQ 0.925 → 0.959 at size 6, bad picks 1 → 0. It costs nothing extra per
     job.
   - It changes the tie-break wording in `contracts/verdict.py` (the `Verdict` docstring) and
     PLAN.md D24. That needs a `contract-change` PR, and it is a selection-side change, so per
     PREREG §13 Nathalie makes it.
2. **Alternative: a more discriminating trial.**
   - Have the Trial Builder generate synthetic invoices that stress the job's hard features (more
     line items, noisier scans), so fewer providers score 100% on the trial and the CIs separate.
   - This attacks the root cause of the ties, but its effect can't be estimated from these logs.
     It costs a re-run to evaluate, and it does nothing for the real-invoice gap on j02.
